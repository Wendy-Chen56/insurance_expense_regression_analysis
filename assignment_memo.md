# Two-Feature Linear Regression Memo

The two-feature linear regression model uses BMI and age to predict medical expenses. The BMI-only baseline model produced an R² of 0.0394, meaning that BMI alone explained about 3.94% of the variation in expenses. After adding age as a second feature, the R² increased to 0.1173. This is an improvement of approximately 0.0779, or 7.79 percentage points, showing that age adds useful predictive information beyond BMI alone. The Normal Equation produced coefficients of approximately -6437.35 for the intercept, 333.39 for BMI, and 241.90 for age. Gradient Descent produced nearly identical coefficients, indicating that both methods converged to the same linear regression solution. The two methods should theoretically agree because they are optimizing the same ordinary least squares objective, although they reach the solution in different ways. The age coefficient, w2, is positive and approximately 241.90. In plain language, holding BMI constant, each additional year of age is associated with about $241.90 higher predicted medical expenses. This coefficient describes an association in the model and should not be interpreted as proof that age directly causes the increase in expenses. Although adding age improved the model, an R² of 0.1173 means that most of the variation in medical expenses is still not explained by BMI and age. Therefore, I would not deploy this model by itself for real-world prediction or business decisions without adding other relevant features and evaluating its performance on unseen data.
